01 / Scope & interpretation

What this guide validates—and what it explains

The workbench is a synthetic bank balance-sheet research system. Its quant engine generates cashflows and risk; the API captures inputs, queues work, and returns tables; the browser edits assumptions and presents results. Understanding a displayed number requires identifying which of those layers produced it.

This document is an implementation-based model specification and validation guide. It explains existing test coverage and proposes additional acceptance work. It does not certify empirical calibration, regulatory compliance, or production suitability. Test names below identify checks present in the repository; their presence alone is not a fresh passing test result.

Four levels of numerical interpretation

Model valuation

Monte Carlo cashflows with base calibration and explicit discounting. Examples: OAS, instrument PV, bumped KRD, nonlinear MBS/deposit stress.

Conditional income

Published macro paths imposed on shared simulated deviations. Outputs are earnings/runoff under the stated narrative, without a pricing-measure interpretation.

Precomputed approximation

Behavioral engines build unit coefficients; allocation changes scale/interpolate them. Forward risk and regulatory quantities retain disclosed simplifying assumptions.

Indicative display

Positions uses product heuristics. It does not become a Monte Carlo result merely because it appears beside other engine outputs.

Coverage source: workspace panel registry; contracts: root instructions and model-assumptions ledger.

Recommended user sequence

  1. Inspect Book Editor and Settings. Confirm the valuation date, balance scale, instrument terms, accounting basis, simulation seed, and horizon. The seed book is proportional synthetic data, not bank-owned positions.
  2. Inspect Market & Scenarios. Confirm decimal/percent conversions, source vintage, tenors, and volatility type. Fetching research data is separate from applying a curve.
  3. Run the base analysis. Obtain Risk Desk and KPI results for one input revision. Price calibration should reproduce target prices; NII should reconcile income less expense.
  4. Ask a specific comparison question. Use a temporary What-if for a contract assumption, a named market shock for valuation sensitivity, or a published forecast for conditional monthly income.
  5. Build Strategy Lab’s library after input changes. Allocate funded new business and inspect monthly constraints, not just today’s tiles.
  6. Optimize if needed. Choose markets and constraints. Check feasibility, independent replay, and funding assumptions before interpreting the allocation or shadow prices.
  7. Retain evidence. Export results with source snapshots, revision, engine version, date, settings, limits, and assumptions. The default backend persists inputs and jobs; keep the run manifest and referenced artifacts together.

Two separate anchors

Pricing anchor: the OAS makes base present value match the saved price. Accounting anchor: the effective book yield and opening carrying value determine accrued income. Neither should be silently refitted to erase the effect of a temporary What-if or forecast.

Three different horizons

The remaining-life simulation supplies up to 360 monthly cashflows for valuation and yield calculations. The report horizon defaults to 27 months and can be shorter. A stress checkpoint h is a future opening balance/state on the unshocked path. A 27-month NII report does not price a 30-year mortgage as if its remaining life were 27 months.

Dates and accrual conventions

Schedule products use adjusted dates from a US bond holiday calendar and a business-day convention (default modified following). If moving forward crosses the month boundary, modified following moves backward. No early-close timing is modeled. The schedule determines accrual fractions and exact payment times; the underlying rate observations are monthly.

Sequential discount-factor bootstrap

At each new pillar T, the engine solves for log D(T) using a bracketed root finder. Intermediate discount factors are interpolated in log space using previously solved pillars and the candidate endpoint. Annual coupon accrual is one year.

Volatility calibration and rate simulation

Three correlated factors

The starting forward correlation is exp⁡(−0.10∣Ti−Tj∣)\exp(-0.10|T_i-T_j|). Eigenvectors for the largest three eigenvalues produce factor loadings B; each row is renormalized to unit length. The resulting correlation is BB⊤BB^\top, a rank-three approximation, not the original full-rank matrix.

At each month the engine emits the short rate and 2-, 5-, 10-, and 30-year par rates before advancing the forwards. Shifted positivity bounds F above −2%. This is a model floor, not a statement that future market rates cannot be lower.

Common random numbers (CRN)

Rate draws pair ZZ with −Z-Z, truncated to the requested path count. Odd path counts are preserved exactly. Primary/secondary spread and HPI draws use separate seed offsets (+101 and +202). Base/up/down evaluations within a run share draws, so differences isolate the input change with much less sampling noise.

Ten key-rate central differences

Each annual par pillar is bumped alone by ±1bp. The curve is re-bootstrapped, rates/cashflows re-generated with shared random draws, and price recomputed with fixed OAS. The abcd parameters stay fixed for curve bumps, although path-dependent product behavior can change.

The total new asset cap limits purchased notional across the allocation, not just peak outstanding balances. Monthly funding uses active opening balances. There are no short positions, integral trade lots, transaction-cost curves, or nonlinear market-impact terms.

Funding is an explicit constraint

Default cash budget is zero: new assets require sufficient new liabilities at every modeled month. A positive budget means additional committed funding outside the base book, available throughout the horizon. It is not permission to count existing reserve cash again. The optimizer does not automatically charge interest on that external budget.

Hand check: a simple funded overlay

$10m of new assets paired with $8m of new deposits has an initial $2m funding gap. It violates a zero cash budget, even if the base bank has ample reserves. A $2m outside-book budget clears only that initial funding row; deposit runoff may create a larger gap later. All modeled months must pass.

Shadow prices and replay

SciPy HiGHS solves a minimization of −t-t. The implementation negates its inequality marginals to report the marginal increase in worst-case NII from relaxing a transformed less-than-or-equal constraint’s right-hand side by one unit. Interpret units and transformed sign carefully: a ratio row’s RHS has currency units; one unit is not automatically one percentage point of the ratio. Multiple binding constraints/degeneracy can make a particular dual unstable.

On solver success, the returned allocation is passed through the Python strategy evaluator for every scenario. It checks the worst-case objective and all monthly LCR/NSFR/EVE/funding limits, horizon capital, asset cap, and commercial amounts before reporting validated=true. This is independent arithmetic replay of the same coefficient model, not a fresh full Monte Carlo valuation of the chosen portfolio.

An infeasible result is meaningful: the modeled limits cannot all be met in the available nonnegative allocation space. The current failure result includes solver message and row labels; that is not necessarily a minimal infeasible subsystem identifying only the conflicting rows.

Validation: use a small LP with hand-verifiable feasibility, include a scenario where base earnings change the worst case, test zero funding, late purchases and maturity, and verify objective/constraint replay. Revalidate after every library/input change.

Job lifecycle and input isolation

Slow work runs through a single-worker job queue because product kernels already parallelize across cores. At submission the API freezes the books/market/histories/settings/assumptions and input revision. Later saved edits do not change a queued job’s inputs. Results are published against the captured identity; interactive strategy evaluation is a separate synchronous calculation over a built, valid library.

Pipeline displays stages, planned versus completed counts, scenario fan-out, and timings. Stage counts can describe logical model work rather than hardware instructions. Cache reuse changes execution cost without changing the mathematical request. Progress percentage and “done” indicate execution status, not financial accuracy.

Retention and restarts

The default backend persists input revisions, jobs, and accepted session journals in SQLite or PostgreSQL, with immutable typed snapshots and Parquet artifacts on local disk or S3. A separate worker runs one heavy calculation at a time per workspace. Unit libraries load after restart; decision sessions rebuild and replay accepted commands before accepting interactive requests. Recovery can be expensive. Published durable outputs do not automatically expire; the former one-hour in-memory retention applies only to the explicit memory execution mode. Preserve the database and referenced artifacts together. See the durable backend guide and architecture map.

The API incremental cache permits 512MiB/500,000 accounted retained nodes; the standalone engine default is 128MiB/50,000. Neither cap bounds in-flight arrays, serialized response copies, allocator overhead, or total process RSS. Cache eviction is safe recomputation. First-call JIT and cold-cache latency should be separated from warm evaluation when benchmarking.

25 / Connected balance-sheet stress

Follow liquidity, credit and capital through time

Open Balance-sheet Stress and run the synthetic bank/dealer example, or edit its explicit JSON specification. Use Load saved-book mapping for explicit six-book integration and optional candidate allocation replay; unsupported saved hedge trades are rejected. Accounts identify legal entity and currency; positions carry accounting classification, credit, funding and collateral assumptions. Netting sets identify counterparties and collateral agreements. Opening equity is supplied and must reconcile.

Scope. This is a deterministic cohort research model. Deposit sensitivities, credit probabilities, market-loss sensitivities and regulatory weights are user assumptions. LCR/NSFR are labeled proxies. No supervisory compliance, empirical calibration or production G-SIB coverage is asserted.

Daily flow and accounting

Every day, the engine settles recoveries, marks securities, draws commitments, migrates and defaults credit exposure, resets allowances, accrues and settles interest, withdraws deposits, rolls or repays funding, books derivative losses and margin, pays operating costs/dividends, executes due management policies, then taxes daily earnings including realized sales. Assets must equal liabilities plus equity at each daily close. Balanced GL entries independently reconstruct closing subledgers and financial statements. Linked collateral principal repays funding; unknown opening pledges remain restricted.

AFS marks enter AOCI; trading marks enter earnings; HTM stays at book value until sale or maturity. AFS sales reclassify OCI without another total-equity loss. Defaults reduce performing principal, establish delayed recovery receivables and consume allowance; only the allowance replenishment affects provision expense. Margin posting swaps cash for a collateral asset rather than recording another trading loss. Interest accrual and cash settlement are separate events.

Policies, local limits and reverse stress

Security sales, secured funding, intercompany transfers and dividend suspension inspect current cash and execute after an explicit lag. Execution rechecks need, cash, unpledged collateral and cumulative limits. HTM sales require explicit policy permission and a face limit. Transfers preserve the donor cash floor and cannot cross currencies implicitly. Outages delay policies; they do not erase contractual outflows. Policies cannot inspect future scenario outcomes.

Baseline and four joint scenarios cover rates/deposit competition, recession credit, dealer margin and confidence/outage. The UI exposes cash and capital paths, first breaches, cohort exposures, action logs and additive event attribution relative to baseline. Reverse stress tests each requested severity without assuming monotonicity or claiming an exact failure boundary. Negative cash represents unfunded obligations; continued projections after failure are diagnostic.
