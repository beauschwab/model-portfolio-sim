# Native supplied deposit state graph

`deposit-product-graph-1` explicitly selects native daily deposit state generation and liability valuation. It leaves existing deposit pricing/calibration routes unchanged. Rust owns supplied-state projection, full-tail discounted cash, base OAS calibration and scenario/bump evaluation. `DepositGraph` retains immutable original base inputs and OAS; `reprice`/`repriced` reject altered base dependency content with `BASE_ANCHOR_CHANGED`.

Each position has complete, ordered scenario histories for physical, pricing, pricing-down and pricing-up roles. Every history supplies a separate strict `DepositStateRequest` and assumption/source identity. Physical histories declare physical measure; the other roles declare pricing measure. All histories retain identical original segment principal, signed accrued rights, paid rate, characteristics, contractual maturity, source revision, currency, actual as-of, full horizon and interest day count. Offered-rate policies, asymmetric lag/half-lives, retention, migration and dated driver observations remain separate supplied inputs in each role. They are not estimated or converted between measures. Q path IDs/order/weights remain paired across scenarios and curve bumps; P histories may have a separate path population. Weights must sum to one without normalization.

The additive shared `deposit_state::simulate_declared_measure` entrypoint preserves explicitly supplied Physical or Pricing identity while using the documented state equations. The original `simulate` remains Physical-only. No P-to-Q model, market-risk premium, empirical fit or logistic plateau is inferred. The underlying model's competing withdrawal/closure/migration allocation, lag observations, destination-rate interest, new inflows and contractual versus assumed terminal payout are documented in [deposit state](deposit-state.md). Daily transitions precede simple interest on resulting destination balances; this is the existing daily allocation convention, not continuous intra-day event-time interest.

## Liability rights and cash ownership

`tail_policy=zero_liability_rights_at_tail` requires every path to end with exactly zero principal and signed accrued-interest rights in every segment. Actual supplied maturities can establish that boundary. Otherwise an explicit `assumed_liquidation` rationale must own final payout. Nonzero `retain_residual` outputs reject; no automatic residual balloon is appended. `franchise_value_only` is rejected because a supplied franchise value cannot stand in for liability cash settlement. The entire supplied horizon is retained and priced even when reporting ends earlier.

Cash is explicitly bank-perspective. Received external principal inflows are positive; repayments and positive-rate paid interest are negative. Graph liability cash is the negative of bank principal-plus-interest cash. Output reports both `liability_pv` and its opposite `bank_cash_pv`. Migration moves principal/accrued state internally and does not become external cash. Recognition expense and cash settlement are separate.

`cash_policy=supplied_bank_perspective_after_opening_actions` prices events strictly after as-of. Same-as-of withdrawal, migration, inflow or maturity effects remain in `opening_action_cash` and retained source states. Callers must reconcile those opening actions before comparing this after-actions value to a book mark. Future new-business inflows and negative coupon receipts are signed cash; they cannot silently be treated as ordinary positive liability runoff.

The positive cash-price OAS target solves against weighted, strictly future, nonnegative liability cash only, in the explicit [-5,5] spread bracket. Signed Q cash requires supplied locked OAS; no monotonic root is assumed. Pricing uses actual elapsed ACT/365 dates, native supplied collateral discount factors and `exp(-OAS*t)` over all tail cash. The base key retains full original Q policies/drivers/openings, curve/source identity, target and graph pairing identity. Scenario edits and reporting changes preserve original OAS; changing the original base Q input requires a new graph. `supplied_history_liability_dv01` is a central native curve bump with separately supplied paired Q histories, not a calibrated behavioral DV01 claim.

Physical reports separately retain signed gross interest expense, bank principal and interest cash, external inflows, withdrawals, closures, contractual maturity, assumed terminal payout and internal principal/accrual migrations. Reports include actual (left,right] periods, opening actions and the full beyond-reporting tail. These are economic expense/cash reports; they do not infer effective-yield NII, booked allowance, journal ownership or FTP. Native source results preserve paid-rate regime flags and all per-segment daily state.

## Admission and evidence

The component admits at most 256 positions, 13 named scenarios, 128 paths per role, 4,096 aggregate supplied histories, 250,000 conservatively admitted/generated rows and eight million work units. Daily segment/migration work is charged before projection; price/root/report passes and complete tails are included. Each core projection retains its 16-segment, 36,600-day and 250,000-work ceilings, subject to smaller graph admission. Raw identity bytes are limited to 32 MiB; conservatively charged retained request/result/key content is limited to 128 MiB. These are component admission bounds, not a whole-process RSS guarantee or 60,000-position scale acceptance. Native cooperative checkpoints run across histories, scenarios, serialization and OAS iterations.

Independent native fixtures verify a 1000 deposit paying 0.365 for two actual days: physical expense 2, bank interest cash -2, contractual principal payout 1000, liability PV 1002 with offset locked discount/OAS. Reporting may stop after day one while PV and source retain day-two payout. Fixtures cover native curve bumps, separately supplied Q rate behavior, original base OAS retention under scenario rate/policy edits, competing migration with once-only accrued-right transport, signed negative coupons, explicit liquidation versus franchise/residual rejection, strict paired identities and exact P-only API compatibility. Synthetic fixtures establish contained mechanics, not empirically observed retention, repricing, stability, regulatory compliance, complete ledger/FTP adoption or cohort-representativeness acceptance.

The [OCC Interest Rate Risk handbook](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/interest-rate-risk/pub-ch-interest-rate-risk.pdf) is a primary modeling reference for separately reviewed deposit repricing, balance decay and non-maturity valuation assumptions. It does not provide this model's coefficients or authorize treating synthetic policies as empirical evidence. Remaining issue #14 gates retain observed customer histories, chronological holdout/regime validation, parameter uncertainty, account/cohort errors and independently verified downstream single-owner ledger/FTP integration.
