# Joint native credit intervals

`portfolio-ledger-native::credit_state` compiles a `credit-state-1` contract and advances immutable `credit-state-opening-1` state with `CompiledCreditState::advance`. Contracts require physical measure, an explicit currency, `currency_units`, source/revision identities, reviewed accounting policy and complete recovery tails. A compiled operator is reusable; coefficients cannot be deserialized or mutated. Opening states retain contract identity and last actual ordinal. Repeating an already recognized date fails contiguous-interval admission.

The interval uses actual elapsed days divided by 365, independently of reporting months. Hazards have inverse-year units; state coupons are signed simple annual rates. The three live states are performing, watch and delinquent; default is retained by immediate exit origin and prepaid is absorbing. The shared operator permits migrations, cures and multiple jumps during an interval. Set the delinquent coupon to zero for nonaccrual; accrued rights already owned by the position continue to transport. A cure starts new earnings only for the time spent in an accruing state.

Probability is absolute population mass, with live/default/prepaid entries totaling at most one. Monetary principal, undrawn commitments, accrued rights and book adjustments are independently weighted aggregate currency amounts. They are never multiplied by population survival a second time. Matured or disposed population can be removed externally, leaving probability submass; maturity is not an economic prepayment.

For row vectors of live principal `B` and accrued rights `A`, the shared operator applies `B'=BQ` and `A'=AQ+BR`, where `Q` is the CTMC generator and `R` is diagonal signed coupon accrual. Earned income is calculated directly from the reward coefficients, avoiding subtraction of two large opening/closing accrued balances. Book adjustment and undrawn commitments use transition transport without invented fee amortization or ordinary draws.

After transport, each default-origin monetary bucket is used as the exact synthetic opening for `credit_accounting`. Separately conserved surviving live balances are merged into accounting's zero-live closing. This permits a surviving accrued right and default right in the same origin to have opposite signs after migration. Netting those rights before allocating default would reject valid signed economics. Origin transfers therefore also work when an origin had no opening exposure. Principal, undrawn, basis, accrued-plus-earned and probability conservation are checked independently; generated postings must reconcile live principal, accrued and basis movements.

Gross default EAD by origin is `D + CCF U`, where `D` and `U` are already transported aggregate default principal and undrawn exposure. The contingent draw funds cash once, increases gross default principal, and cancels all defaulted undrawn commitments. Principal-only economic loss is `LGD EAD`. The complete recovery amount is `EAD - loss`, distributed using supplied strictly dated lag weights. Default and recovery lags are recognized from the interval's closing day. This remains an explicit boundary-time approximation; it does not assign stochastic intra-day cash settlement times.

Default principal transfers to its separate asset account. Economic loss does not automatically create a provision, allowance, chargeoff or recovery receivable. Allowance targets and chargeoffs require the explicit dated reviews defined by `credit_accounting`; default accrued reversal and basis recognition follow its reviewed policy. Existing claims and full recovery tails are retained through principal maturity. Intervals crossing an unsettled recovery date must be split, so no scheduled recovery silently disappears. Daily use with `interval_days=1` naturally resolves every date.

Economic prepayment settles its transported principal and signed accrued right in cash, recognizes transported signed basis in earnings and cancels the prepaid undrawn allocation. This contract's prepayment policy is full settlement of those rights. It does not represent fees, retained contractual accrued rights or partial debt forgiveness; a producer requiring those economics must supply a distinct explicit boundary policy. Negative coupons and cash payable rights are admitted rather than clipped.

Bounds: outgoing row hazards and signed coupon magnitudes reuse the shared operator's 100/year limits. Intervals admit 1..36,500 actual days; daily integration uses one day. Each origin has at most 32 recovery points, lags at most 36,600 days and weights summing to one within eight machine epsilons. LGD and CCF are finite fractions in 0..1. Empty tails require LGD one. Currency amounts reuse the 1e15 limit; claims and reviews reuse accounting's 62,000 and 4,096 limits. Identity fields admit at most 256 UTF-8 bytes, claim namespaces 128, control characters are rejected, and metadata reuses the shared 32 MiB bound. These component limits are not an aggregate process memory guarantee.

Independent tests use analytic competing default/prepay flux and earned-interest integrals, a performing-to-watch-to-default chain, opposing signed migrated rights, zero-credit mass/money independence, exact retained absorbed probability, multi-day recovery claims, explicit allowance/chargeoff reviews and failed-input immutability. They validate numerical and accounting mechanics, not empirical calibration accuracy or accounting compliance. The native production replay and independent persisted-journal verification must separately prove adoption before issue #11/#12 acceptance.

Model references: the Federal Reserve's [2025 supervisory model descriptions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-june-supervisory-stress-test-methodology-descriptions-supervisory-models.htm) describe credit-state transitions and EAD including expected draws; its [January 2026 detailed credit documentation](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/files/credit-risk-models.pdf) distinguishes model default thresholds and projected losses. These references motivate separate transition, EAD and severity components; they do not calibrate these supplied coefficients. [Reviewed credit accounting](credit-accounting.md) documents the distinct recognition and chargeoff policies.
